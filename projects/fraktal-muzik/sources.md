## Deliberately NOT claimed
- That the **Mandelbrot set or the Koch curve "contain" music.** Both are used only as **visual metaphors** for zoom and self-similarity. Sonifications exist, but they are mappings chosen by an artist, not music hidden in the math.
- That **all music is fractal**, or that 1/f proves "music = fractal". The finding is **statistical** scale-invariance over a limited range (down to about the length of the piece).
- That Shepard tones really rise without end. It is an **illusion**.
- That Nørgård used our **diatonic (scale-degree) mapping**. His series is defined in semitone steps from G. We map it onto **scale degrees** of G dorian/lydian (stated in render_brief; open choice for the user).

## Honest answer to the title
**In a sense, yes.** You can write melodies that **literally contain themselves** at every slower tempo: Nørgård's infinity series (1959) is the same melody when you take every 4th note, forever. Real music also has **statistically fractal-like (1/f)** fluctuations of loudness, pitch and rhythm. But the "infinite" part lives in the **rule**, not in a piece you can hear to the end: any performance is finite, and the "endless rise" we feel (Shepard) is a trick of the ear.
