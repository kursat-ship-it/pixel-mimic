## Deliberately NOT claimed
- That **Libet proved free will is an illusion**. On screen it is "EVET?" with a question mark, followed at once by the 2012 noise reinterpretation.
- That a revealed prediction **must** fail. Scriven's argument is contested (Lewis & Richardson), so the captions say "ŞAŞABİLİR / TUTABİLİR" (can).
- That **quantum randomness gives (or kills) free will**. It is posed as a question; the plan gives both sides.
- That **"the future is fixed"** is settled physics. Laplace's picture is classical; quantum mechanics and chaos complicate prediction in different ways.

## Honest answer to the title
**Most philosophers would say: probably yes, but "seçim" means something more modest than magic.**
- Even if the future were fixed and someone knew it, you would still be the one weighing reasons and deciding. For compatibilists (≈ 59 % of philosophers in 2020), that *is* choosing.
- The real snag is **you** knowing it. A prophecy told to you becomes one of the causes of your choice. If you're the "tersini yaparım" type, a revealed prediction can fail (Scriven). A prediction can stay certain only if it is kept from you (MacKay).
- So perfect foreknowledge and your choosing can coexist, but a perfect prophecy **in your hands** can't simply be read and obeyed.
- Hard determinists disagree (choice is an illusion either way). Libertarians think real choice needs an open future.
- Neuroscience hasn't settled it either: Libet 1983 was challenged by Schurger 2012.
